Breakdown Recovery on the M5

The M5 heads south-west out of Birmingham, meeting the M6 near Great Barr before running past West Bromwich and Dudley on its way through the Black Country towards Worcester and beyond. We recover breakdowns anywhere along this corridor, with our fastest response nearest the M6 interchange, West Bromwich and Dudley.

A road we know well close to Birmingham

The M5 is a long route — it eventually reaches Exeter, well over a hundred miles from Birmingham — but our coverage is based in Birmingham and the West Midlands, so we're honest about where we're strongest. Breakdowns near the M6 interchange, past West Bromwich and on towards Dudley are within easy reach and normally our quickest jobs. Further out towards Worcester or Gloucester, we can still often help, but call and give us your exact location so we can tell you plainly whether we're the right recovery option or whether you'd be better served locally.

The M5/M6 interchange

This section carries a genuinely heavy mix of commuter, freight and through traffic, and roadworks or lane restrictions aren't unusual here. If you break down close to the interchange, the merging lanes and multiple slip roads can be confusing at speed, so pull onto the hard shoulder as early and as fully as you safely can rather than trying to reach a specific junction. When you call, mention the interchange itself, a nearby junction number, or Great Barr by name — it helps us find you quickly among several similar-looking slip roads.

Towards West Bromwich and Dudley

South of the interchange, the M5 passes close to West Bromwich and on towards Dudley, carrying steady commuter traffic in and out of the Black Country alongside longer-distance journeys. Hard shoulder availability varies along this stretch, with some sections using variable speed limits and all-lane running at busier times — treat any lane as potentially live unless clearly signed as a hard shoulder or refuge.

Driving — and breaking down — on a managed motorway

Parts of the M5 around Birmingham use smart motorway technology, meaning the hard shoulder isn't always a hard shoulder — check the overhead gantries for a red X before assuming a lane is safe to stop in. If you do need to stop, use a marked emergency refuge area where one is available, or get as far left as possible and put your hazards on immediately. Watch and listen for the sound of approaching traffic before attempting to exit the vehicle, and get behind any barrier as quickly as you safely can.

On managed sections without a continuous hard shoulder. If you break down in a live lane and can't reach a refuge area, stay in the vehicle with your seatbelt on and hazard lights flashing, and call for help immediately — in an emergency, dial 999. If you can get out and safely reach a place away from the carriageway, do so, staying behind any barrier until help arrives.

Something other than a full recovery?

If your problem might be fixable at the roadside — a flat battery or a puncture, for example — our roadside assistance service may get you moving again without a full tow. Tell us what's happening and we'll advise on the best option.

Questions

M5 Breakdown FAQs

Do you cover the whole M5, including down towards Worcester and beyond?

Our coverage is Birmingham and the West Midlands, so we're strongest on the M5 near West Bromwich and Dudley. If you've broken down further south towards Worcester or Gloucester, call us anyway and tell us your exact location — we'll be upfront about whether we can reach you or whether a more local recovery operator would get to you faster.

What should I do if I break down near the M5/M6 interchange?

This is one of the busiest interchanges in the country, often with a mix of lane closures and merging traffic. Get as far onto the hard shoulder as you can, keep hazards on, and mention the interchange or the nearest junction number specifically when you call so we can find you among the various slip roads.

Is the M5 through the Black Country busier than other stretches?

The section between the M6 interchange and Dudley carries a heavy mix of commuter and freight traffic, and can back up at peak times. If your vehicle is showing signs of trouble, try to reach the next hard shoulder refuge or junction while it's still safe to keep driving, rather than waiting until you're forced to stop.
