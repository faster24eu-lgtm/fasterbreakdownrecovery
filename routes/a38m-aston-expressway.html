Breakdown Recovery on the A38(M) Aston Expressway

The Aston Expressway is the direct route into Birmingham city centre from Spaghetti Junction on the M6 — a short, elevated dual carriageway with reversible tidal-flow lanes. If you've broken down anywhere along it, we can recover you.

A road built to move traffic fast into the city

The Aston Expressway carries the A38(M) designation for roughly two miles, running from Spaghetti Junction (the Gravelly Hill Interchange on the M6) directly into Birmingham city centre at Old Snow Hill. It's genuinely distinctive among the roads in this guide — seven lanes wide, with the middle three reversible depending on the time of day, generally running inbound during the morning peak and outbound in the evening. That tidal-flow system means the road behaves differently depending on when you're on it, and it's worth mentioning the approximate time when you call.

Breaking down on an elevated, high-speed approach road

Because the expressway is elevated for much of its length, verges and hard-standing are limited, and there's no realistic way to pull off the road entirely in most places. If your vehicle starts to give trouble, ease off early and try to reach one of the limited refuge areas or the next exit rather than continuing to hope the problem resolves itself. If you do come to a stop in a live lane, this is one of the situations where getting yourself and any passengers out of the vehicle and to a place of safety — rather than remaining inside — is usually the right call, given the speed and volume of traffic.

At the Spaghetti Junction end

The northern end of the expressway feeds directly into the Gravelly Hill Interchange, where the road markings, gantries and multiple levels can make it hard to describe your exact position. A junction number, a sign you can see, or a nearby landmark such as Aston or Newtown all help us place you accurately.

At the city-centre end

Towards Old Snow Hill, the expressway comes down to meet Birmingham's Middleway ring road and the edge of the city centre proper. Traffic here mixes with slower-moving local roads and pedestrian crossings, so a breakdown at this end behaves more like a city-centre call-out than an open-road one — getting into a side road or car park just off the main route, if you can do so safely, may be a better option than stopping on the expressway itself.

If you break down on the Aston Expressway: because there's little to no usable verge on most of the elevated sections, prioritise reaching a refuge area, exit or the next safe stopping point while the vehicle is still driveable. If you're forced to stop in a live lane, get yourself and any passengers out and to a safe position away from the carriageway rather than remaining inside the vehicle. Keep hazard lights on at all times, and tell us the approximate time of day so we know which way the tidal-flow lanes were running.

If you've had a collision rather than a breakdown

If your vehicle isn't driveable following a collision on the Aston Expressway, our accident recovery service handles that separately from a standard breakdown — call us and let us know it was a collision so we can plan accordingly.

Questions

A38(M) Aston Expressway Breakdown FAQs

What are the tidal-flow lanes on the Aston Expressway, and does that affect recovery?

The Aston Expressway has seven lanes, with the middle three reversible depending on the time of day — mostly inbound towards the city in the morning and outbound in the evening. It's worth telling us the time of day and which lanes were running which way when you called, since that can affect how a recovery vehicle safely reaches you.

Is the Aston Expressway the same road as Spaghetti Junction?

Not quite — the Aston Expressway is the roughly two-mile stretch of elevated dual carriageway running from Spaghetti Junction (the Gravelly Hill Interchange on the M6) into Birmingham city centre at Old Snow Hill. Spaghetti Junction itself is the interchange at the expressway's northern end.

Do you cover breakdowns right into the city centre end of the expressway?

Yes — we cover the full length of the A38(M), from Spaghetti Junction down to where it meets the Birmingham Middleway near Old Snow Hill. Give us your direction of travel and the nearest gantry or landmark when you call.
