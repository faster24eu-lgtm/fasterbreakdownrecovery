Breakdown Recovery on the A96

The A96 heads north-west out of Aberdeen past Dyce and Aberdeen Airport, on through Inverurie, Huntly and Keith towards Elgin and Inverness. We recover breakdowns anywhere along this corridor, with our fastest response nearest Aberdeen, Dyce and Inverurie.

A road we know well close to Aberdeen

The A96 is a long route — it eventually reaches Inverness, over sixty miles from Aberdeen — but our coverage is based in Aberdeen and Aberdeenshire, so we're honest about where we're strongest. Breakdowns near the city, past Dyce and Aberdeen Airport, and on towards Inverurie are within easy reach and normally our quickest jobs. Further out towards Huntly, Keith or Elgin, we can still often help, but call and give us your exact location so we can tell you plainly whether we're the right recovery option or whether you'd be better served locally.

The Dyce and Aberdeen Airport stretch

This section carries a busy mix of commuter, freight and airport-related traffic, and roadworks or lane restrictions aren't unusual here. If you break down close to the airport, junctions can be confusing at speed, so pull off as early and as fully as you safely can rather than trying to reach a specific roundabout. When you call, mention the airport, a nearby roundabout or business park, or the Dyce area by name — it helps us find you quickly among several similar-looking access roads.

Towards Inverurie — where the road narrows

Beyond the immediate Aberdeen approach, the A96 becomes a mix of dual and single-carriageway sections as it heads towards Inverurie. Traffic speeds stay high even where the road narrows to one lane each way, and overtaking manoeuvres on the single-carriageway stretches mean vehicles can be travelling close to the centre line. If you break down here, don't assume there's more room than there is.

Driving — and breaking down — on a rural single carriageway

Much of the A96 outside the immediate city area is single carriageway, and that calls for different thinking to a motorway-style breakdown. There's no central reservation, so get the vehicle fully onto the verge rather than just the edge of the lane, and check the ground will actually hold the car's weight before you commit — soft verges after rain are common in Aberdeenshire. Make yourself visible: a high-vis vest, if you have one, and hazard lights are far more important here than on a well-lit urban road, particularly around dusk or on an overcast day when passing traffic sees you later. Watch and listen for farm vehicles and livestock movements, which use this road more than most of the A90, and give them room. If there's no safe verge at all, it's often better to keep going gently to the next passing place or field entrance than to stop in a genuinely blind spot.

Rural stretches after dark. Away from Aberdeen and Dyce, sections of the A96 have no street lighting. If you break down at night, put your hazards on straight away, use a torch or phone light to make yourself visible while you move to a safe position off the road, and stay well back from the carriageway edge until we arrive.

Something other than a full recovery?

If your problem might be fixable at the roadside — a flat battery or a puncture, for example — our roadside assistance service may get you moving again without a full tow. Tell us what's happening and we'll advise on the best option.

Questions

A96 Breakdown FAQs

Do you cover the whole A96, including near Elgin and Inverness?

Our coverage is Aberdeen and Aberdeenshire, so we're strongest on the A96 near Aberdeen, Dyce and Inverurie. If you've broken down further up the route towards Huntly, Keith, Elgin or Inverness, call us anyway and tell us your exact location — we'll be upfront about whether we can reach you or whether a more local recovery operator would get to you faster.

What should I do if I break down on the A96 near Aberdeen Airport?

The stretch near Dyce and the airport carries a mix of local, airport and through traffic, often with roadworks or diversions. Get as far onto the verge as you can, keep hazards on, and mention the airport, a nearby roundabout, or the Dyce area specifically when you call so we can find you among the various access roads.

The road is single carriageway where I've stopped — is that more dangerous than a dual carriageway breakdown?

It brings different risks rather than automatically more of them. There's no central reservation to separate you from oncoming traffic, verges can be narrow or soft, and overtaking traffic may be travelling close to the centre line. Get the vehicle as far onto the verge as the ground allows, make yourself visible, and stand well back from the road edge while you wait.
