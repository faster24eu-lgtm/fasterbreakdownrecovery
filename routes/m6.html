Motorway recovery

Breakdown Recovery on the M6

The M6 is Britain's longest motorway, running about 230 miles from the M1 near Rugby, through Birmingham and the north-west, over Shap and on to the Scottish border beyond Carlisle. Wherever you've stopped on it, call us with the nearest junction and direction and we'll send a local recovery operator to you.

One motorway, very different stretches

The M6 starts at the Catthorpe interchange with the M1 near Rugby and finishes just short of Gretna, where it becomes the A74(M) into Scotland. In between it changes character completely: a congested urban motorway through Coventry and Birmingham, a busy freight corridor through Staffordshire and Cheshire, the Thelwall Viaduct over the Manchester Ship Canal, and then the long, exposed climb through the Lune Gorge and over Shap in Cumbria. Where you've broken down makes a real difference to how a recovery is planned, so the more precisely you can tell us your location, the better.

Coventry, Birmingham and Spaghetti Junction (junctions 2–10)

Through the West Midlands the M6 carries some of the heaviest traffic in the country. It passes Coventry, meets the M42 at junction 4, and then runs through the Gravelly Hill Interchange at junction 6, better known as Spaghetti Junction, where it crosses the A38(M) Aston Expressway on several levels of flyover. North of there it passes Great Barr, meets the M5 at junction 8 near West Bromwich and runs on through Walsall. There's often very little verge on the elevated sections, so getting fully out of the running lane matters even more here. Our Birmingham page has more on recovery in and around the city.

The M6 Toll

The M6 Toll splits away near junction 4 and rejoins near junction 11, bypassing the busiest part of the Birmingham section. If you break down on the toll road, tell us that you're on the M6 Toll rather than the main M6: the two run some distance apart, and a recovery vehicle sent to the wrong one loses time.

Staffordshire and Cheshire (junctions 11–20)

North of Cannock the motorway runs past Stafford and Stoke-on-Trent and through Cheshire towards Warrington. Long stretches here carry a heavy flow of lorries heading to and from the north-west, and speeds stay high. Services at Hilton Park, Stafford, Keele, Sandbach and Knutsford are useful landmarks if you know which ones you've passed.

Warrington to Lancaster (junctions 20–35)

The Thelwall Viaduct between junctions 20 and 21 carries the M6 high over the Manchester Ship Canal and the River Mersey, and the M62 crosses the M6 at Croft (junction 21a). Further north the motorway passes Wigan, Preston and Lancaster, with links to the M58, M61 and M55 to Blackpool. These busy interchanges can mean confusing slip roads, so mention the junction you last passed and your direction of travel when you call.

Cumbria and Shap (junctions 36–45)

North of Kendal the M6 climbs through the Lune Gorge and over Shap, reaching more than 1,000 feet above sea level. This is one of the most exposed stretches of motorway in England: high winds, snow and fog are common in winter, and junctions are far apart, so it can be a long way to the next exit. If you break down up here, stay well away from the carriageway, keep warm, and tell us the nearest junction or the services you last passed (Tebay or Southwaite are common reference points).

If you break down on the M6: move as far left as you can, onto the hard shoulder or into an emergency area if there is one. Put your hazard lights on, leave the vehicle by the left-hand door and wait behind the barrier, well away from traffic. If you've stopped in a live lane and can't get out safely, stay in the car with your seatbelt on and call 999. Then call us with the junction numbers either side of you and your direction of travel.

Smart motorway sections

Several parts of the M6, including stretches around Birmingham, in Staffordshire and Cheshire, and between Warrington and Wigan, are smart motorways where the hard shoulder is used as a running lane. On these sections, look for an emergency area (marked with an orange surface and an SOS sign) and watch the overhead signs for a red X. Our guide to breaking down on a smart motorway explains what to do step by step.

How recovery on the M6 works

When you call, we take your location, vehicle details and destination, then send a local recovery operator from the nearest available area. On a motorway that also means being towed to a safe place off the motorway, or straight to the garage or address you choose. We'll give you an honest estimate of how long it will take before anyone sets off.

Questions

M6 Breakdown FAQs

How do I tell you where I am on the M6?

Look for the blue driver location signs on the verge (they show the motorway, direction and a distance number), the last junction number you passed, or the last services. Tell us your direction of travel too, for example northbound towards Preston. If you're on a smartphone, you can also share your location with us on WhatsApp.

I've broken down on the M6 Toll. Can you still recover me?

Yes. Just tell us clearly that you're on the M6 Toll and not the main M6, along with the nearest junction, because the two roads run several miles apart through Staffordshire and the West Midlands.

Do you cover the whole length of the M6?

Yes. We arrange recovery anywhere on the M6, from the M1 at Rugby to the end of the motorway near Gretna. Response times depend on where the nearest available recovery operator is, and we'll give you an estimate when you call.

I've broken down at Spaghetti Junction. What should I do?

Spaghetti Junction (the Gravelly Hill Interchange, junction 6) has several levels of flyover with little verge space. Get as far left as you safely can, put your hazards on and, if you can leave the car safely, wait behind the barrier. Tell us which road and direction you were on, and any sign you can see.
