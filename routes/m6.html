Breakdown Recovery on the M6

The M6 is the motorway that defines the Birmingham conurbation — running up from the M42 near Coleshill, through Spaghetti Junction and Great Barr, past the M5 interchange, and on through Walsall towards Staffordshire. If you've broken down anywhere on this stretch, we can recover you.

A motorway that defines the conurbation

Few roads shape Birmingham's geography the way the M6 does. It skirts the north-east of the city through the Gravelly Hill Interchange — universally known as Spaghetti Junction — before running on past Great Barr, meeting the M5 near West Bromwich, and continuing north through Walsall's own junctions towards the M6 Toll split and Staffordshire beyond. Traffic here rarely lets up, and the road carries everything from local commuters to long-distance freight, so knowing roughly which stretch you're on helps us judge how much space you realistically have and plan the quickest way to reach you.

Breaking down around Spaghetti Junction and Gravelly Hill

The Gravelly Hill Interchange is one of the most complex junctions in the country — multiple levels of flyover carrying the M6 over and under the A38(M) Aston Expressway and a web of local roads. If your vehicle starts to falter here, it can be genuinely difficult to know exactly which level or slip road you're on, so look for any junction number or sign you can see and tell us your direction of travel. There's less usable verge space on the elevated sections than on the open motorway further out, so getting fully clear of the running lane matters even more here.

Great Barr and the M5 interchange

Heading north-west from Spaghetti Junction, the M6 passes Great Barr before meeting the M5 near West Bromwich — a busy interchange in its own right, with a steady flow of traffic peeling off in both directions. If you break down on the approach to this junction, indicate early, ease off the throttle gradually rather than braking hard, and aim for the nearest hard shoulder or slip road rather than trying to nurse the car all the way to a specific exit.

Through Walsall towards Staffordshire

North of the M5 interchange, the M6 runs through Walsall's own set of junctions before the M6 Toll splits away and the road continues towards Staffordshire and beyond. Traffic thins out a little compared with the Birmingham stretch, but speeds stay high, so the same principles apply: get off the running lanes as early as you safely can, and keep your hazards on the moment you suspect a problem.

If you break down on the M6: get the vehicle as far off the running lanes as you safely can — onto a hard shoulder, verge or layby. Once stopped, get yourself and any passengers out of the vehicle and behind a barrier, well away from moving traffic, rather than waiting inside the car if it's still close to the live lane. Keep your hazard lights on at all times. Don't attempt any roadside repair in a live lane — it isn't worth the risk. When you call for help, give us the nearest junction number, exit or a landmark, plus the direction you were travelling, so we can find you quickly.

If you've had a collision rather than a breakdown

If your vehicle isn't driveable following a collision on the M6, our accident recovery service handles that separately from a standard breakdown — call us and let us know it was a collision so we can plan accordingly.

M6 Breakdown FAQs

I've broken down at Spaghetti Junction — what do you need to know?

Spaghetti Junction (officially the Gravelly Hill Interchange) is a complex, multi-level junction where the M6 meets the A38(M) Aston Expressway and several local roads. Tell us which level or slip road you're on if you can see a sign, plus your direction of travel — there's often less run-off space on the flyovers than on the open motorway, so getting fully clear of the traffic lane matters just as much, if not more.

Is it safe to wait in my car if I've broken down on the M6?

It depends on where you've stopped. If you're on a verge, in a layby, or otherwise well clear of the running lanes, staying in the vehicle with your seatbelt on is usually the safer option while you wait, especially in poor weather. If you're still partly in a live lane and can't move further off safely, get yourself and any passengers out and behind a barrier or well away from the carriageway, then call for help.

Do you cover the M6 all the way to Staffordshire, or just the Birmingham stretch?

Our strongest coverage is the Birmingham conurbation — Spaghetti Junction, Great Barr, the M5 interchange and through Walsall — but call us anyway if you're a little further out and we'll confirm how quickly we can reach you.
