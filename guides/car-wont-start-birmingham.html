Car Won't Start in Birmingham? What to Do Next

If your car won't start, don't waste time trying to diagnose a complicated fault yourself. Call and explain what happened. We can help work out whether you need a jump start, roadside assistance or recovery.

What should I do if my car won't start?

The most useful first step is almost always the same one: call us and tell us what happened. You don't need to work out what's wrong before you pick up the phone — that's what we're there to help with.

  • Stay safe first — if you're anywhere near moving traffic, get yourself and any passengers somewhere safe before doing anything else.
  • Tell us what happened — whether the car failed while parked or while you were driving makes a real difference to what's likely going on.
  • Give us your location, even an approximate one to start with — we can help pin it down more precisely once we're on the phone.
  • If the engine is making a serious mechanical noise, don't keep turning the key or revving it to see what happens — stop and call us instead.

From there, we'll ask a few simple, practical questions to get a sense of what type of assistance is likely to be appropriate — not to diagnose the fault with certainty, but to send the right help.

What the Symptoms Can Tell Us

None of this is a guaranteed diagnosis — we can't examine the vehicle from the other end of a phone call. But what you describe often gives us a useful idea of what's likely, and what kind of help to send.

No power at all

If you come back to the car and there are no dashboard lights and nothing happens when you turn the key, that can often point towards a battery or electrical power issue — though it isn't always that simple.

Dashboard lights on, engine won't start

If the car has power but won't start, that can have a number of different causes. It's not something we'd want to pin down with certainty from the phone, but it's worth telling us so we can plan the right response.

Car stalled while driving

A vehicle that was driving normally and suddenly stalled, rather than gradually losing power, may point to a mechanical, fuel, injection or electrical fault. Recovery is often the more appropriate option here.

Knocking, grinding or rattling

A sudden knocking, grinding or severe rattling sound from the engine is a sign to stop, not to keep driving or revving to see what happens. Continuing to run a mechanically damaged engine can make things worse.

Clutch pedal to the floor

If the clutch pedal suddenly goes to the floor with no resistance, that may indicate a clutch or hydraulic problem, and the vehicle will likely need recovering rather than driven further.

Goes into gear but won't move

If the engine runs and the car goes into gear but doesn't actually move, or gear selection feels wrong, that may indicate a clutch, gearbox or transmission problem, and recovery is usually the safer route.

Flat tyre

Depending on the situation, we can take the vehicle to an appropriate tyre shop on a recovery truck, or a mobile tyre service may be a suitable option instead — we'll talk through what fits best.

Keys locked inside

Depending on the vehicle and situation, this might involve a locksmith or a recovery operator with suitable vehicle-entry equipment. Tell us the vehicle make and model and we'll advise on the best route.

Out of fuel

Fuel delivery can be arranged where applicable. Let us know your location and we'll talk through the options.

Keep it simple

What We Need to Know When You Call

A handful of straightforward questions — nothing you need to prepare for in advance.

  • Where the vehicle is right now.
  • Where you'd like it to go — home, a garage, a dealership or elsewhere.
  • The make and model of the vehicle.
  • What happened, in your own words.
  • Whether the vehicle rolls freely or the wheels are locked.
  • How many passengers are with you.
  • How urgent the situation is.
Not sure which you need?

Do I Need a Jump Start or a Recovery Truck?

The two solve different problems, and it isn't always obvious which applies until someone's talked it through with you.

Jump start may be appropriate

Where the issue appears to be battery-related — no power at all, or a slow crank rather than nothing — a jump start may get you moving again without needing a tow.

More on roadside assistance →

Recovery is often the safer option

Recovery tends to be the more appropriate route where the vehicle stalled while driving for no obvious reason, the alternator may have failed, there's a suspected mechanical fault, the engine is making serious noises, the clutch or gearbox isn't functioning properly, or the vehicle simply can't move safely.

More on breakdown recovery →

A quick note on jump starts: even if a jump gets the engine running, a failed alternator means the battery isn't being properly recharged as you drive, so the vehicle can stop again shortly after. If that's a possibility, recovery is often the more reliable choice from the outset.

If you're not somewhere safe

Car Won't Start on the M6 or M42

A car that won't restart after stalling on a fast road needs a different approach to one that won't start on your driveway. Safety comes first, before anything else.

  • 1

    If it's safe to do so, put your hazard lights on immediately so other drivers can see you.

  • 2

    If the car has stopped in a live lane and you can't move it, get yourself and any passengers out and well away from moving traffic, behind a barrier if there is one.

  • 3

    Don't keep attempting to restart the engine repeatedly while exposed to fast-moving traffic — get somewhere safe first, then call.

  • 4

    Once you're safe, call us with your nearest junction, exit or landmark and your direction of travel.

This applies whether you're on the M6 itself, the M42, or the A38(M) Aston Expressway into the city. For general breakdown cover in the area, see breakdown recovery in Birmingham or, if the fault might be fixable on the spot, roadside assistance.

Where Can the Vehicle Go?

Once the vehicle's been recovered — or if a jump start or roadside fix isn't the answer — you can normally tell us where you'd like it taken. That might be your usual garage, a dealership, home, or somewhere else that suits you. We'll take it there as part of the job.

Questions

Car Won't Start FAQs

My car has no power at all. Is it the battery?

No power at all — no dashboard lights, nothing when you turn the key — can often point towards a battery or electrical power issue, but we wouldn't want to guarantee that over the phone. Tell us what happened and we'll help work out whether a jump start is worth trying or whether the vehicle needs recovering.

My car stopped while I was driving. Do I need recovery?

If the vehicle stalled while you were driving normally, rather than losing power gradually, that can indicate a mechanical, fuel, injection or electrical fault rather than a simple flat battery, and recovery is often the safer option. Call us and describe what happened and we'll advise.

Can you jump-start my car?

Where the issue looks like a straightforward flat battery, a jump start may be appropriate. If the alternator has failed, though, a jump start might get the engine running again only for it to stop once more because the battery isn't being charged properly — so recovery can be the more reliable option in that case.

What if my engine is making a knocking noise?

If the engine develops a knocking, grinding or severe rattling sound, stop the engine rather than continuing to drive or revving it to see what happens — running a mechanically damaged engine can make the damage worse. Call us and we'll arrange recovery.

My clutch pedal is on the floor. Can you recover the car?

Yes. A clutch pedal that suddenly goes to the floor can indicate a clutch or hydraulic problem, and the vehicle usually isn't safe to drive in that state. We can recover it to a garage or your chosen destination.

My car goes into gear but doesn't move. What should I do?

That can point to a clutch, gearbox or transmission problem. Don't keep trying to force it into gear — call us, tell us what's happening, and we'll arrange recovery.

I don't know my exact location. Can you find me?

Yes. If you're not sure exactly where you are, we can talk you through sharing your location, including sending a live location via WhatsApp, so we know where to send the recovery vehicle.

What information do I need to give you?

Just the basics — where you are, where you'd like the vehicle to go, the make and model, what happened, and whether the vehicle rolls freely or the wheels are locked. You don't need a diagnosis before you call.
