Buying advice

A Recovery Man's Guide to Buying a Reliable Car

If you want to know which cars break down most, don't ask the salesman. Ask the person driving the recovery truck. At Faster we load up cars that have given up on the road every week. This is what we see, make by make, and what we'd buy ourselves.

In short: if you really want to break down, buy a Range Rover. If you'd rather never meet us, buy something simple: a well-looked-after Volkswagen Golf or Polo, or a Japanese petrol car. For a van: a Mercedes Sprinter that you look after properly.

To be upfront: this is our experience from everyday recovery work, not a scientific study. What we recover also depends on what's driving around in our area. But after years on the road you see patterns you'll never read in a brochure.

The top 3 we have on the truck most often

1. Land Rover and Range Rover

Number one by a distance. We see the newer models far too often: engine damage, electrical faults, gear selectors that refuse to play, warning messages nobody can make sense of. Name a part and we've seen it fail. A Range Rover is a lovely thing to drive. In our experience it just spends a bit too much time following our truck instead of driving itself.

Big reliability studies back this up: in J.D. Power's Vehicle Dependability Study, Land Rover sits in the lower half.

2. Jaguar

We recover Jaguars less often, but when one stops it's rarely anything small. Usually it's major engine damage or a complicated electrical fault that needs a real specialist just to diagnose. Not the kind of breakdown your local garage sorts out in an afternoon.

3. BMW

That one almost goes without saying. Lovely engines, great to drive, but a lot of technology and a lot of electronics, so a lot that can go wrong. The more complicated a car is, the more likely it is to end up on our flatbed one day.

Vans: this is where it hurts

With vans, two groups keep coming back:

  • Stellantis vans (Peugeot, Citroën, Fiat, Vauxhall): lots of breakdowns, parts that don't last, and engines with a timing belt that runs in oil, a so-called wet belt. On the group's 1.2 PureTech petrol engine, that belt wore out so early that Stellantis itself extended the warranty to 10 years or 175,000 km.
  • Ford Transit and Transit Custom: wet belt trouble here too. On the 2.0 EcoBlue diesel, Ford itself cut the belt replacement interval from 10 years or 150,000 miles to 6 years or 100,000 miles, because diesel gets into the oil and attacks the belt. The risk is highest with lots of short trips, typical of delivery work.

Our favourite: the Mercedes Sprinter. On one condition: you treat it right. Service it on time, don't put things off, don't keep driving with a warning light on. A well-maintained Sprinter is, in our view, the best van you can buy.

The safest choice: a Volkswagen

Volkswagen says it themselves: Das Auto. A good Golf or a good Polo is, in our experience, the safest choice if you just want your car to keep going. Why? Because they're simple. Volkswagen doesn't reinvent the wheel with every new model. The basics have been proven for years, and simple engineering means less to go wrong, especially compared with a BMW.

Yes, they're boring cars. But it's not about who has the coolest car. It's about who never has to call for recovery.

Petrol or diesel, big or small

  • Petrol: go Japanese. Toyota, Honda, Mazda and co. are top tier for us. Studies from J.D. Power and Consumer Reports also put Lexus and Toyota among the most reliable brands.
  • Diesel: go German, preferably a Volkswagen, as long as you look after it.
  • Not too small: very small engines, especially petrol, don't last as long in our experience. We'd go for at least a 1.6, and for diesel something along the lines of the old 1.9.
  • The simpler, the better. Every extra turbo and every extra electronic system is one more thing that can break.
  • Second tier: French makes and Ford. Not bad, but not where we'd look first.

What about hybrids and electric cars?

Honestly, it's too early to judge. Germany's motoring club ADAC sees fewer breakdowns per 1,000 cars for two- to four-year-old electric cars (3.8) than for petrol and diesel (9.4). But the small 12-volt battery is still the biggest cause of breakdowns there too. In the ADAC's 2025 statistics, some Toyota hybrids such as the Yaris Cross even stood out, because of that small 12-volt battery. No engine damage, but still stuck.

Your first car? A Volkswagen Polo 1.2 petrol

Lots of people are looking for their first car. Our advice is simple: a Volkswagen Polo with a 1.2 petrol engine. Above we usually say at least a 1.6, but for a first car we make an exception. A first car mostly does shorter trips, and a small petrol engine keeps fuel costs and often the insurance down. For a new driver, that matters.

  • Simple: not much complicated technology, parts are everywhere and every garage knows it.
  • Easy to learn in: small, easy to see out of and easy to park.
  • Pick one with a full service history and have it checked before you buy.
  • Don’t confuse it with the 1.2 PureTech from Peugeot, Citroën and Vauxhall: that’s a different engine, with the wet belt we warn about above.

Tips when buying used

  • Ask for the service history and invoices. A car that's been serviced on time lasts much longer.
  • Wet belt? Ask for proof the timing belt was changed on time, especially on a 1.2 PureTech or a Ford EcoBlue.
  • Check all the electrics: windows, screens, sensors and any warnings on start-up.
  • Test drive an automatic and watch for jerks or hesitation when it changes gear.
  • Get it inspected by an independent garage before you sign.

And if you do break down, or your automatic is stuck in Park, read car stuck in Park: shift lock release by make, or just call us.

Questions

Reliable Car FAQs

Which makes does Faster recover most often?

In our everyday work, mainly Land Rover and Range Rover, then Jaguar and BMW. With vans we often see Stellantis vans and the Ford Transit and Transit Custom.

What is the most reliable car according to a recovery driver?

We'd buy a well-maintained Volkswagen Golf or Polo, or a Japanese petrol car. Simple engineering means less to go wrong.

What is a good first car?

We'd recommend a Volkswagen Polo with a 1.2 petrol engine: simple, economical, easy to learn in and often cheap to insure. Pick one with a full service history.

What is the best van?

For us, the Mercedes Sprinter, as long as you service it properly and on time.

What is a wet belt?

A timing belt that runs in the engine oil. On some engines it wears faster than expected, such as the Stellantis 1.2 PureTech and the Ford 2.0 EcoBlue.

Are electric cars more reliable?

According to the ADAC, young electric cars have fewer breakdowns per 1,000 cars than petrol and diesel. The 12-volt battery is still the most common cause of breakdowns.

Is this an official statistic?

No. It's our own experience as a recovery service, backed up with figures from public studies where they exist.
