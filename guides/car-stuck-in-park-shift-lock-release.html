Car Stuck in Park? How to Get an Automatic Into Neutral

Flat battery, gear selector stuck in P, and the car won't move an inch? Most automatics have a hidden override, but where it is varies by make, and plenty of garages don't know. This is what our recovery drivers actually do, make by make.

In short: try a jump start first. With power, almost every automatic goes into N normally. Without power, you need a mechanical override: on many cars a small slot next to the gear selector, on Jaguar and Land Rover a lever in the centre console or engine bay. Cars with a purely electronic selector or a robotised gearbox have no mechanical override, so the car is loaded onto skates (dollies) instead.

Safety first

As soon as the park lock is released, the car can roll, even on ground that looks flat. Before using any override:

  • Chock the wheels front and back.
  • Apply the parking brake if it still works, or have someone hold the brake pedal.
  • Nobody stands in front of or behind the car while it is released.
  • Use the override only to load or reposition the car, not to tow it along the road.
  • Reset it afterwards, so Park works again.

Why the selector is stuck in P

In Park, a pawl inside the gearbox locks the driven wheels. To stop anyone knocking it out of Park by accident, the selector only releases when the ignition is on and the brake pedal sends an electrical signal. This is the shift lock. With a flat battery there's no signal, so the lever stays in P. Many newer cars with a rotary dial, push buttons, a small toggle or a column stalk have no mechanical link to the gearbox at all.

Step 1: try power first

The easiest override is a charged battery. Jump start the car with leads or a booster pack, switch the ignition on, press the brake and select N. On many cars N only holds while the ignition stays on. See flat battery: jump start or recovery?

The four types of override

1. Shift lock release slot next to the selector

On many cars with a traditional lever there's a small cover next to or below the selector, often marked "Shift Lock".

  1. Chock the wheels and hold the brake.
  2. Prise off the small cover with the key blade or a thin flat screwdriver.
  3. Push the key or screwdriver down into the slot.
  4. Keep pressing, press the brake pedal and move the selector to N.
  5. Refit the cover once the car is loaded.

2. Release lever or cable (Jaguar, Land Rover)

Jaguar and Land Rover use a separate release lever. Depending on the model it's under a trim panel in the centre console, under the cupholder, or in the engine bay. Usually you turn a lock through 90 degrees and pull the lever up until it latches. Details are in the table below.

3. Electronic selector with no mechanical link

Toggles, rotary dials, buttons and column stalks only talk to the gearbox electronically. Without power there's no override a driver can use, so it's a jump start or skates.

4. Robotised (automated manual) gearboxes

A robotised gearbox is a manual gearbox with motors doing the clutch and gear changes. With no power it often stays in the last gear selected. Examples: Fiat Dualogic and Comfort-Matic, older Smart, Citroën and Peugeot ETG or EGS, Renault Quickshift. N is only possible with power, otherwise skates.

Electric parking brake: can it be released?

An electric parking brake also needs power to release. Some early systems had a mechanical emergency release, for example in the centre console or the boot. Newer cars have no release a driver can use, so the car goes on skates. Garages can open the brake with a diagnostic tool or directly at the motor, but that isn't a roadside job.

When nothing moves: skates and lifting

If the car won't go into N and the parking brake won't release, we use skates (dollies). They slide under the locked wheels so the car rolls even though the wheels don't turn. In tight spots like underground car parks, or after an accident, we lift the car instead. For some BMW and Mercedes models there's also a freewheel tool that bolts to the wheel hub and lets the car roll at walking pace with the gearbox locked. Never drag a car on locked wheels, it damages tyres, gearbox and drivetrain.

Tips from our recovery drivers

  • The override is often missing from the handbook or very well hidden. On Jaguar and Land Rover, look under "vehicle recovery".
  • People look for the Jaguar release in the centre console, but on all-wheel-drive models it's in the engine bay.
  • On a Range Rover Sport hardly anyone spots the lever under the cupholder. Take the rubber base out first. If it isn't there, it's in the engine bay under the air filter.
  • If nothing works, skates are faster and kinder to the car than forcing anything.

Questions

Car Stuck in Park FAQs

How do I get an automatic into neutral with a dead battery?

Use the mechanical override if the car has one: usually a small slot next to the selector, or on Jaguar and Land Rover a lever in the centre console or engine bay. Often a jump start is easier, then the selector works normally.

Why won't my gear selector come out of Park?

The selector only releases when the ignition and brake pedal send an electrical signal. A flat battery or a faulty brake light switch stops that, so it stays in P.

Where is the shift lock release?

On many cars, under a small cover next to or below the selector, sometimes marked "Shift Lock". The exact spot is in the handbook.

Does every automatic have a shift lock release?

No. Many newer cars with an electronic selector, rotary dial or column stalk have no mechanical override. Then N only works with power.

Can I tow an automatic once it is in neutral?

Only if the manufacturer allows it, and only short and slow. The override is mainly meant for loading. A flatbed is always the safer choice.

What are car skates or dollies?

Small wheeled trolleys that slide under locked wheels, so a car can be moved and loaded even when the gearbox or parking brake is locked.

Do I need to reset the override afterwards?

Yes. Otherwise Park won't hold and the car can roll away. On a Jaguar, for example, return the lever to horizontal and turn the lock back.
